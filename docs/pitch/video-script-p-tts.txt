Pippon has three parts: an iPhone app that reads your My Number Card, a check that any website can run in its own browser page, and a Solana program for one-per-person gates. Here is each of them, live.

This is an online store selling sake. At checkout it needs one fact: is the buyer 20 or over? The store shows a QR code. I scan it with my iPhone, and the pippon app shows who is asking and what they will learn. I tap my real My Number Card on the phone. The app reads the chip and builds a zero-knowledge proof on the device, so the card data never leaves the phone. The store's page checks the proof by itself: no pippon server, no account, no wallet. It learns three things: 20 or over, the prefecture, and an ID that only this store sees. It never gets my name, my birthday, my address or a photo of my card.

The second place a proof can go is Solana. This gate keeps its rules on-chain: 20 or over, and one claim per person. I connect a wallet, the app makes a proof that only works for that wallet, and I press Mint. Our program, zee-kay gate, verifies the proof on-chain, records that this card has claimed, and mints the NFT, all in one transaction. The same card cannot claim here again from any wallet, and nobody keeps a list of people. The store and the gate read the same list of trusted government keys from Solana, so both trust the same root.

The iPhone app is in TestFlight beta, with a demo card for people who do not have a My Number Card. The web demo is live, and all the code is open source. Pippon: prove the fact, not your ID.
