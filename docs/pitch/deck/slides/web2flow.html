Flow 1 · Web2 · online store selling alcohol

How the store learns "20+" without seeing your ID

STORE

1 · Checkout asks

Shopper taps "Verify age with p!pon". The store makes a one-time session ID and shows it as a QR code (a link on mobile).

PHONE

2 · Request opens

The p!pon app shows who asks and what: "Kura Online Store · Age 20+".

CARD

3 · Tap the card

After the signing password, the chip signs SHA-256(session ID ‖ nonce) and gives its J-LIS-signed certificate.

PHONE

4 · Prove on device

Groth16 proof (~9.5 s): CA signature, card signature, age ≥ 20, prefecture. The certificate never leaves the phone.

STORE'S BROWSER

5 · Verify, ~200 ms

Proof valid · same session (no replay) · CA in the trust list on Solana · dated today · 20+ · scoped to this store.

Store keeps: age 20+ = yes, prefecture, a store-only pseudonymous ID.

Never receives: name, date of birth, address, ID image, My Number.

Once per account or order, from home — not a card scan at a shop counter.

Here is the Web2 flow, for an online store that sells alcohol. At checkout the shopper taps "Verify age with p!pon". The store creates a one-time session ID and shows it as a QR code, or a link on mobile. The p!pon app opens the request and shows who is asking and for what. The holder enters the card's signing password and taps the card: the chip signs a challenge made from the session ID and a nonce, and hands over its government-signed certificate. The phone then builds a Groth16 proof that checks both signatures, the age and the prefecture; the certificate never leaves the phone. The store's browser verifies the proof in about 200 milliseconds: valid proof, same session, trusted CA from the list on Solana, today's date, 20 or over, scoped to this store. The store keeps a yes, the prefecture and a store-only pseudonymous ID — nothing else.
