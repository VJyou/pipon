Roadmap · product design · Web2

Verify at sign-up. After that, just log in.

ONCE · AT SIGN-UP

1. Tap the card; the proof is bound to the store's sign-up session.

2. The store verifies it and saves on the account only: "20+ verified on (date)" and its store-only pseudonymous ID.

EVERY TIME AFTER · USUAL LOGIN

3. Log in with a password or passkey, as today.

4. Checkout reads the flag on the account: alcohol can be sold, no ID asked.

Nothing to leak

A breach exposes a yes/no flag, not a licence image.

One account per person

The pseudonymous ID blocks duplicate sign-ups.

Re-check when it matters

Yearly, or before a high-risk order, by the store's policy.

Or reuse across stores

A store can also accept the Web3 attestation: no scan at all.

Works with today's verifier: the store keeps the result of one proof instead of a copy of the ID.

And the Web2 product: an online store verifies you once, at sign-up. You tap the card, the proof is bound to the store's sign-up session, and the store saves only "20 or over, verified on this date" and its store-only pseudonymous ID. After that you log in as usual, and checkout just reads the flag. If the store is breached, attackers get a yes-or-no flag, not a licence image. The pseudonymous ID blocks duplicate accounts, the store can re-check yearly or before a high-risk order, and a store can also accept the Web3 attestation so returning customers don't scan at all. This already works with today's verifier.
